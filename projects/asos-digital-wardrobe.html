ASOS: Can Digital Twins Cut Returns and Build Loyalty?

A digital fitting-room and wardrobe concept, powered by digital twin technology, designed to tackle ASOS's two hardest commercial problems with a single product mechanism.

The Challenge

ASOS serves around 20M customers across 200+ markets, but has faced two connected problems since turning unprofitable in 2022. Up to 50% of its returns are classified as unnecessary, driven by size and fit issues rather than genuine dissatisfaction, with poor fit the single leading cause, ahead of every other reason combined, and online return rates running at three times or more the level of brick-and-mortar retail. Each return costs ASOS an estimated £20 to £30. ASOS also operates in a highly competitive, fragmented market where customers can switch to a competitor in a click, making loyalty hard to earn and easy to lose.

The Approach

We proposed a digital fitting room and wardrobe feature, powered by digital twin technology: customers build a virtual body and style profile once, then preview fit and styling across ASOS's catalogue before buying, directly targeting the size-and-fit issue behind most returns. Zalando has already shown the ceiling here, cutting return rates by up to 40% with a less advanced version of the same idea.

Build a digital twin profile
Preview fit before buying
Fewer size-driven returns
Stickier, loyalty-building habit

The same mechanism doubles up on loyalty: a digital wardrobe creates positive network effects through styling history, saved outfits and personalised recommendations that raise the cost of switching to a competitor without that data on you. We mapped the risks, adoption, data trust, integration cost, and a mobilisation plan using vendor subsidies and customer incentives to get customers and vendors to critical mass, rather than treating this as a slide-only idea.
