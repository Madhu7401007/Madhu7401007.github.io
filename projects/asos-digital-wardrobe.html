ASOS — Can Digital Twins Cut Returns and Build Loyalty?

A digital fitting-room and wardrobe concept, powered by digital twin technology, designed to tackle ASOS's two hardest commercial problems with a single product mechanism.

RoleGroup Project Analyst
ContextImperial College Business School — Strategy & Innovation in Digital Business
TimeframeApr 2025
SectorDigital Business / Retail Tech

The Challenge

ASOS faces two connected problems. Up to 50% of its returns are classified as unnecessary — driven by size and fit issues rather than genuine dissatisfaction, with poor fit the single leading cause, ahead of every other reason combined. ASOS also operates in a highly competitive, fragmented market where customers can switch to a competitor in a click, making loyalty hard to earn and easy to lose.

The Approach

We proposed a digital fitting room and wardrobe feature, powered by digital twin technology: customers build a virtual body and style profile once, then preview fit and styling across ASOS's catalogue before buying — directly targeting the size-and-fit issue behind most returns.

Build a digital twin profile
Preview fit before buying
Fewer size-driven returns
Stickier, loyalty-building habit

The same mechanism doubles up on loyalty: a digital wardrobe creates positive network effects — styling history, saved outfits, personalised recommendations — that raise the cost of switching to a competitor without that data on you. We mapped the risks (adoption, data trust, integration cost) and a mobilisation plan to get customers and vendors to critical mass, rather than treating this as a slide-only idea.

The Outcome

A structured business case connecting one product mechanism to two of ASOS's most material commercial problems — built for Imperial's Strategy & Innovation in Digital Business module with a five-person team.
